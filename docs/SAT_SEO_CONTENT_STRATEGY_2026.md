# NITAQ ACADEMY — SAT SEO & CONTENT STRATEGY
## Google Trends–Driven Plan for UAE / Sharjah / Dubai
**Prepared for Management Review • October 2026**

---

### Purpose
Use Google Trends demand signals to improve Nitaq Academy's existing SAT pages, create targeted informational articles where genuine gaps exist, and build a repeatable SAT content system that supports both organic visibility and student enquiries.

---

### 1. Executive Summary
The SAT search landscape in the UAE is showing two different types of demand: high-intent searches around preparation, coaching, registration and dates, and rapidly rising informational searches around grammar, Desmos, Bluebook, question banks, formula sheets and upcoming test dates. The strategy should therefore not be based on placing every keyword on the course page.

- **Commercial/local intent** should remain concentrated on the Sharjah and Dubai SAT course pages.
- **Diagnostic intent** should remain concentrated on the public SAT Diagnostic page (`/sat/diagnostic`).
- **Informational/rising searches** should be captured through the existing SAT article library and targeted new articles.
- **Academy/brand searches** such as *Bedrock SAT*, *Khan Academy SAT*, and *One Prep SAT* should be handled as comparison/alternative-resource content—not as direct claims or keyword stuffing.
- **Date-driven demand** should be handled through a maintainable SAT dates/resource page or article rather than repeatedly editing multiple course pages.
- **Data Interpretation**: The percentage figures are Google Trends growth signals, not absolute search volume. Google Trends reports relative search interest and rising-search growth; a 'Breakout' label indicates growth above 5,000%.

> **Management Takeaway**:  
> The opportunity is not simply to rank for 'SAT'. The objective is to own the UAE SAT search journey:  
> **Discover → Understand → Practice → Assess → Compare Options → Enquire / Enrol.**

### 2. Google Trends Data: How We Interpret It
The source dataset is the UAE Google Trends SAT research covering a recent 24-hour view, a 3-month view, and rising/breakout queries. Google Trends growth percentages indicate change in search interest relative to the comparison period.

| Signal | What it means for Nitaq | How to use it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Past 24 hours** | Very recent demand / seasonal trigger | Use for timely updates, dates, registration and urgent FAQs. |
| **Past 3 months** | More stable demand direction | Use for content priorities and on-page optimization. |
| **Rising %** | Acceleration in interest | Use to identify article opportunities and emerging topics. |
| **Breakout** | >5,000% growth according to Google Trends | Investigate topic; do not assume large absolute volume. |
| **Irrelevant term** | Trend is not SAT education intent | Exclude from SEO strategy. |

### 5. Competitor & Resource Term Positioning (Bedrock, Khan Academy, One Prep)

| Search Interest | Do | Do Not |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bedrock SAT (+3,950%)** | Create an objective *"SAT Prep Options in the UAE: What to Compare"* or a carefully researched comparison article. | Do not claim superiority or make unsupported statements about Bedrock. |
| **One Prep SAT (+2,800%)** | Compare preparation approaches/resources using transparent criteria. | Do not imitate or keyword-stuff their brand name. |
| **Khan Academy SAT (+20%)** | Create a practical guide: official free resources + when students may need structured coaching. | Do not present Khan Academy as a competitor without evidence or make unsupported performance claims. |
| **College Board / Bluebook** | Explain official resources and how Nitaq preparation complements them. | Do not imply Nitaq is affiliated with College Board. |

**Recommended Positioning**:  
Nitaq positions itself as the guide that helps students understand the SAT ecosystem—including official tools and popular preparation resources—then provides a structured preparation pathway. This builds topical authority and trust without attacking competitors.

### 8. Implementation Rules
1. Use exact Trends wording when natural in titles, H2s, FAQ schema, image alt text, or prose.
2. Use semantic variants elsewhere to avoid repetitive stuffing.
3. Keep one clear primary intent per URL (Commercial on landing pages, Informational in articles).
4. Use internal links: `Article → Diagnostic → Course Landing Page`.
5. For date queries, maintain a single canonical dates resource updated each test cycle.
6. Validate rising trends as directional signals, not absolute volume.
7. Avoid unsupported claims regarding competitors, official affiliations, and guarantees.
